Exhibit 4 · Why enforcement is the design

Three times we asked nicely. Three times it took a refusal.

While hardening 3.0’s assumptions feature, we ran a planted-ambiguity benchmark, patched the method, and re-ran — three cycles. The same law fired every time:

Probe 1 — the sweep ships

Docs ask for every API surface and honest unknowns. The run enumerated surfaces — then answered every question as confident fact and bundled three unknowns into one line.

prose rule → routed around

Probe 2 — the register moves into the scaffold

Each line now starts from “the request does not say…”. The frame held, unbundled — and the run collapsed five endpoints into a single surface, shrinking the sweep from ~25 questions to 5.

new prose rule → routed around again

Probe 3 — freeze refuses a collapsed surface

“One surface per S id” becomes an engine refusal. The run enumerated five clean surfaces, and the sweep caught a genuinely missed question (order:S5) live — refusing the freeze until it was answered.

engine checkpoint → held

Prose rules get routed around; engine checkpoints hold. That result — reproduced three times for $7.84 in agent time — is why 3.0 converts guidance into refusals instead of adding more documentation. It is also exactly what the ledger above shows about 2.5.

Exhibit 6 · Entered against ourselves

What 3.0 does not claim

3.0 makes agents ask the right questions. It cannot make them give the right answers. On two of seven planted items the agent chose the wrong reading in every run, before and after every improvement. No artifact checker can fix that. What 3.0 guarantees is that the guess is on the record, priced, at the moment you approve — auditability, not correctness. That is the release claim, verbatim.

Also on the record: this comparison found two real 3.0 bugs — a missing tamper seal (a regression from 2.5) and a parser flaw that silently disabled it. Both were found by driving the artifacts, fixed with failing-tests-first, and pinned. A method that audits itself should show its scars.
