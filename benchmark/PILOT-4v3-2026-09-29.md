## The environment moved

The same `vanilla` wm1 cell cost $0.40 / 0.42M tokens / 7 turns on 09-28 and $1.02 / 2.49M / 33
turns on 09-29; the first turn's context grew from ~46k to ~58–65k tokens. Cost and turns are
therefore only comparable within one day. The 09-28 numbers are in PILOT-4v3-2026-09-28.md.

## What it shows

- **The oracle is saturated; the new dimensions separate runs.** Oracle 1.00 in 17 of 18 runs, but
  edge robustness ranges 8–19 and mutation score 0.50–0.88.
- **Current vs previous 4.0, same day:** amb1 — held-out edges 10.3 vs 8 of 14 and mutation 0.78 vs
  0.58, at 2.3× the cost; wm1 — same edges, mutation 0.64 vs 0.50, cost +17%, turns about equal.
- **4.0 vs no method on wm1:** 4.0 held every typed-input edge (19/19 in 2 of 3 reps; vanilla 15/19)
  but its own tests were weaker at catching seeded bugs (0.64 vs 0.83; vanilla wrote 38 tests).
- **Evidence honesty held everywhere it could be checked: 15/15 ADD runs.** The EVIDENCE suite count
  matched a fresh rerun of the final tree every time.
- **The defects the edge suite found are real:** a JSON `null` or number body crashes the server
  (5xx) in five of nine 09-29 apps, a non-object body is accepted, and `priority: true` passes an
  integer check because `bool` is an `int`.
- **The one oracle miss** (current 4.0, wm1 rep2) returned `GET /bookings` as a wrapped object, not
  a list — a defensible reading of "list" that the oracle does not accept.
- **Cost:** the second-reader subagent fires on floor work (both workloads were read as data or
  security), and one amb1 run spawned three subagents. That — and the turns it adds — is the main
  cost lever to test next.
