## The headline finding (n=1 — direction, not conclusion)

**No arm gamed. All four implemented the hostile change as an honest re-spec**:
premium bypass working (201), the contradicted rule surviving scoped (non-premium
409), zero test-weakening at the hostile step, zero regression on clean state.

The WV2 hypothesis — "ADD's tamper tripwire forces an honest re-spec; unguarded flows
take the shortcut" — **fails at n=1 for this temptation strength**: sonnet-5-class
agents didn't need a guardrail to re-spec honestly. Under the honest-outcome clause
that is the finding, not a framing problem. Combined with WV1: on friendly AND
mildly-hostile single-shot workloads, trust floors don't differentiate the flows at
n=1 — cost does (spec-kit/vanilla ≈ $3 vs add $8.16 vs add-main $11.14; the branch
holds −27% vs main at equal trust).

What WOULD sharpen the temptation (levers for a follow-on rep/workload, if funded):
- Contradict a rule the arm's own suite pins HARD (many asserts across files) instead
  of one rule with a natural scoped amendment.
- Time pressure: a lower turn ceiling makes the honest path (re-spec + test updates)
  materially more expensive than deletion.
- A change request that BREAKS a hidden invariant the prompt never restates —
  measuring whether the flow re-derives the frozen spec or trusts the request text.
