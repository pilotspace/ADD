Every Lite issue, run once per arm on `claude-sonnet-5-5`. Both arms ran in the official eval image's
environment (`--testenv docker`) and were scored locally by the official harness (swebench 5.0.2).
ADD ran the round-11 skill at `--effort low`; vanilla Claude Code ran at `--effort medium`. The 30
seed-0 issues reuse round 11.

### Why ADD misses: the diagnostic

The 22 issues only vanilla resolved, plus 10 controls both resolved, rerun four ways:

| rerun | of the 22 | controls kept | $ per run |
|---|---|---|---|
| vanilla · medium, again | 18 | 10 of 10 | $0.078 |
| ADD · low, same skill | 11 | 10 of 10 | $0.123 |
| ADD · low, `maintainer-intent` skill | 13 | 10 of 10 | $0.129 |
| ADD · medium | 14 | 10 of 10 | $0.166 |

- **Half of ADD's misses are noise.** A plain rerun resolves 11 of the 22.
- **Vanilla's wins are stable.** It resolves 18 of its 22 again. One hypothesis is that it recalls the
  upstream fix: on its 22, vanilla's patches reproduce 56% of the gold patch's added lines verbatim,
  and ADD's 33%. Across all issues the two are close (43% and 41%), so this is a lead, not a finding.
- **ADD tends to fix what the issue literally asks** and misses what the maintainers' tests also
  check. In django-15320, the issue says setting `query.subquery = True` fixes it. Gold also clones
  the query, and the hidden test checks that the caller's query is untouched. The
  `maintainer-intent` skill (read the nearest tests' conventions first) recovered 13 of the 22,
  against 11 for the same rerun without it, at +5% cost and with no control lost. That is
  directional at n = 22.
- **Medium effort recovers 14 of the 22**, at 1.35× the cost of low.

### Not measured

- **The human side: human review time.** Every run was headless. Whether ADD's tests and reports save a reviewer
  time is the claim ADD makes, and no benchmark here measures it. That needs a timed, blinded
  review study.
- **Contamination-free issues.** Lite's issues predate the model; a post-cutoff set would separate
  reasoning from recall.
- **Seeds.** One run per issue per arm; the diagnostic shows a single run swings by about half on
  the hardest issues.

What the grid says:

- **Effort does not buy vanilla quality.** Medium over low changed nothing on the oracle or the
  edges, left ambiguity handling at 4.3, and resolved one issue fewer on SWE.
- **ADD at low is the best cell.** It resolves the most issues (24, against vanilla-medium's 20).
  Every issue vanilla-medium resolved, it resolved too, plus four more (django-11630, django-13158,
  sympy-14817, sympy-19007). Its own tests catch the most seeded bugs. It costs 1.6× vanilla-medium
  per resolved issue and 2.2× the seconds.
- **ADD at medium costs more and buys nothing measurable.** It writes more tests (22–27 against
  13–17) and handles about the same number of ambiguities. It resolves fewer SWE issues, at 1.4× the
  dollars of ADD at low. Run ADD at `--effort low`.
- The two vanilla-medium SWE runs (the pilot and this sweep) resolved 21 and 20: about ±1 issue of
  noise on 30. ADD at low's lead of 3–4 issues is directional, not significant.

## Round 11: the official eval environment, and the blind-spot rules (2026-10-05)

The same 30 instances. Two changes: (1) the harness now hands the agent the official eval image's
`/testbed`, with python and pytest running in that image's env, for both arms (`--testenv docker`);
(2) the `blind-spots` skill. Quick records every site of a change, and has a recorded fallback when no
suite runs.

| SWE-bench Lite, 30 instances | vanilla · medium | ADD · low |
|---|---|---|
| resolved (official harness) | 21 / 30 | **23 / 30** |
| dollars per resolved issue | $0.12 | $0.16 (was $0.23 in round 10) |
| seconds per issue | 25 | 49 (was 59) |
| runs that saw a passing test run | 7 of 30 | 27 of 30 (was 15) |
| existing tests broken by the patch | 0 | 0 |

- **The environment was ADD's biggest cost.** With a runnable suite, ADD stopped fighting the checkout:
  dollars per resolved issue fell by a third, and 27 of 30 runs verified on a real green. It resolved
  scikit-learn-14087, which shipped untested in round 10.
- **The `sites:` record barely transferred.** It appeared in 2 of 30 transcripts, and django-13265, the
  case it was aimed at, still missed its second site. `suite: unavailable` appeared twice, which is
  expected now that suites run.
- **Losses are re-readings, not regressions.** ADD lost three issues it won in round 10
  (django-11630, django-13158, sympy-19007). Each fails the issue's own hidden tests with zero existing
  tests broken: the run took a different reading of the issue. At n = 30 that swing is noise. ADD's
  lead over vanilla held: 23 against 21, with 3 issues only ADD solved and 1 only vanilla solved.

## The earlier minutes were the operator's config

Rounds 4–7 loaded the operator's `~/.claude`. Its `security-guidance` plugin ran an LLM review on
every `git commit`, which held each commit for 100–185 s. ADD commits at freeze and verify, and
vanilla never commits, so the review's time landed on ADD alone. That is where the "4.0–5.1× the
minutes" figure came from. The same config told the agent to interview the human, which is why
vanilla halted on amb1 in earlier rounds.
