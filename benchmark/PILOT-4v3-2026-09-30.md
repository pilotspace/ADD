# Pilot round 4 — what ADD 4.0 buys Claude Code, and what it only costs (2026-09-30)

Arms: `add-4` at 0ec8b2d5 (the skill after close-benchmark-gaps: every rule covered, cheap guesses
checked, subagents only for security, a malformed-input check) and `vanilla`. Same harness, same
day, `claude-sonnet-5 --effort medium`, wm1 + amb1, **n = 3 per arm per workload. Not significant.**
Runs: `benchmark/runs-4v3-2026-09-30/` (gitignored). Quality: `python -m benchmark.quality`.

## Disclosures

- **Four add-4 runs died on a 403** (`oauth_not_allowed_for_organization`) when the account changed
  mid-run. They were set aside (`runs-4v3-2026-09-30-failed403/`) and rerun at the same commit the
  same day. One rerun's first attempt was cut off mid-verify with no result event; the second attempt
  finished it. Its cost ($2.02) is the recorded $0.28 plus the first attempt's 4.58M tokens at the
  $0.38/M of its sibling runs — an estimate.
- **"vanilla" is not bare Claude Code.** The harness passes no `--setting-sources`, so both arms load
  the operator's `~/.claude`: a CLAUDE.md that asks for red/green TDD and plugin hooks with workflow
  rules. Every delta below is ADD over *a disciplined user config*, which likely understates the gap
  to bare Claude Code. Both arms, and round 3, ran under the same config.
- **Beats are split at commits.** Direction runs to the `freeze(` commit and Verify starts at the
  `verify(` commit, so Verify's seal diff and fresh run happen before its commit and count as Build.

**One escaped defect.** add-4 amb1 rep0 crashes `DELETE` on any timezone-aware timestamp
(`TypeError: can't subtract offset-naive and offset-aware datetimes`, `app/store.py:120`). Its own
tests sent naive timestamps, so rule, check and code agreed and were all wrong — the same-author
blind spot `references/evidence.md` §1 describes. That one crash is the whole amb1 oracle miss and all
three of that run's "neither" ambiguity verdicts (every cancel/promotion probe needs a working
DELETE). add-4 held the cancel oracle in its 6 earlier amb1 runs (09-28, 09-29).

## Cost ledger — where the 2.2–2.7× goes

| beat | share of context tokens | assistant turns | tool calls per run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction (to `freeze(`) | **46%** | 37–47 | 24 — 10 Write, 7 shell, 3 test runs, 2 Read |
| Build (to `verify(`, including Verify's checks) | 51–53% | 34–39 | 22 — 5 shell, 5 Edit, 4 test runs, 4 git, 3 Write |
| Verify (the commit) | 1–3% | 1–2 | < 1 |

SKILL.md says "Direction = two turns". It takes about forty. That gap between what the skill asks and
what happens is the largest single cost in the method, and the practice that pays for itself (the
falsifier) is a small part of it.

## What to optimize — value per cost, ranked

1. **Keep the falsifier and the red-first seal.** They are the only practice with a consistent
   measured gain, and they cost a few test runs.
2. **Make Direction actually short.** Grounding in one shell command, then the task file, tests and
   stubs in one turn. Direction is 46% of tokens; halving it would take roughly a quarter off the
   total without touching a check.
3. **Add a "shape" sweep to the checks: inputs the way a real caller sends them.** That means the
   body itself (null, a number, not an object, not JSON) and values in every form the spec allows
   (timezone offsets, encodings). Both escapes this round — garbage bodies and naive timestamps — are
   one class: the check's inputs shared the builder's picture of the input. The garbage-body half
   overlaps the held-out edge suite (teaching to the meter); the timezone half is off-meter.
4. **Make ASSUMPTIONS earn their tokens.** Today they change no decision. Rank them by blast radius
   and lead the report with the riskiest one ("any caller may cancel any booking") so the human
   review can act — or write fewer.

oracle is not saturated.
