## Finding 1 — the intervention changed what ADD writes, not what it builds

The `## ASSUMPTIONS` section (freeze refuses a template slot) moved surfacing
0.143 → 0.333 and left defensible landings flat (5/7 → 5/7 mean). Runs recorded 5–7
substantive assumptions each and still shipped silent decisions. The release claim for
v3.0.0-beta.1 is therefore **auditability only** — the guess is now on the record where
a reviewer can disagree with it — and no correctness claim.

## Finding 2 — two scorer defects, both pushing the same direction

Both were found by partitioning all 21 post-intervention item-runs by failure stage
(named at all? → marker+anchor in one sentence? → pre-edit? → attributable?), and both
penalised document-first methods — the same directional bias as the three defects fixed
in the 2026-07-26 campaign (`test_ambiguity_meter_fixes.py`).

1. **The vocabulary missed the declarative register** (fixed 2026-08-10,
   `test_marker_variants.py`). MARKERS was drawn from chat-style hedging; a method that
   writes its recognition into a document *states* it (`never restricts`, `not stated`,
   `doesn't define`). Three real, pre-code, in-artifact surfacings went uncredited on
   morphology alone. Every addition is a morphological variant of an existing marker;
   baselines did not move.

2. **The first-match race** (fixed 2026-08-11, `test_surfacing_search.py`).
   `_find_surfacing` returned the *first* marker+anchor pair and `classify` vetoed it if
   attribution assigned it to a sibling — never continuing to an attributable sentence
   further down. ADD's dense opening assumption line (waitlist + 409 + priority +
   position in one sentence) won the race for several items and was vetoed for most,
   while clean dedicated sentences below it were never reached. Recovers exactly 2 of 16
   post-run misses (`A-priority-vs-fifo` in rep1 and rep2). Baselines did not move.

Also fixed, claiming **mechanism only**: `_SENT_SPLIT` treated a markdown bullet ending
without punctuation as sentence-continuation, fusing whole `## ASSUMPTIONS` blocks into
one mega-sentence. Measured before merging: this recovers **zero** item-runs on every
archived run — the glued block's pairs are unattributable even once split. An earlier
in-session estimate attributed ~43% of the loss to it; that number counted the vetoes
the glue *touches*, not the misses it *causes*, and is corrected here.

Scoring-side, the same campaign fixed **authored-artifact filtering**
(`test_authored_artifacts.py`): the workspace sweep scored vendored/installer prose as
the arm's own words, which had manufactured a false surfacing advantage for the add arm.

## Finding 3 — why 7/7 is not reachable by better free-association

The 16 post-run misses partition (under the pre-fix meter):

```
2   meter: first-match race                (now fixed)
~5  attribution ceiling: one dense line can credit at most one item
~9  method: the recognition was never written at all
```

The method misses are not noise — they are concentrated:

| item | asks | result across ALL 5 runs |
|---|---|---|
| `A-list-scope` | **whose** bookings does GET /bookings return | 0/5 — never surfaced, never guessed right |
| `A-cancelled-visibility` | **which** — do cancelled rows appear | 0/5 surfaced |
| `A-adjacent-boundary` | **when** — is back-to-back a conflict | 0/5 surfaced |
| `A-position-ordering` | **order** of the queue | 0/5 surfaced |
| `A-conflict-response` | the 202-vs-409 **contradiction** | 5/5 surfaced — free, every arm gets it |

A contradiction is loud; gaps and traps are silent. Free-association follows the spec's
own emphasis (status and conflict are discussed constantly; caller identity is named
once), so every run wrote assumptions about the loud POST path and none about the quiet
GET path. The meter's real floor is 0/6, not 0/7.

## The prediction now on record (pre-registered before the next run)

The surface × dimension sweep (`gives:` scaffolded; freeze refuses unswept
`(dimension, surface)` pairs; landed post-campaign as 6083b67c) would have refused all
three post reps with `who:S2 · which:S2 · when:S2 · order:S2` unswept on the GET
surface — mechanically forcing a line about each of the four never-surfaced items, and
its one-line-per-dimension scaffold breaks the dense-A1 style that loses the attribution
race. It targets 14 of the 16 misses.

Success criteria for the n=1 probe, written before the run:

- **primary**: `A-list-scope` or `A-cancelled-visibility` named in ≥1 rep (currently
  0-for-5 across all arms — any hit is signal);
- **secondary**: surfaced > 2.33/7 (the post-arm fixed-meter mean);
- **guard**: defensible ≥ 5/7 and oracle 1.0 — the sweep must not cost correctness;
- **honesty**: if surfacing rises and landings fall, the finding is "writes more,
  builds the same", reported as such.

The previous prediction in this campaign (that `## ASSUMPTIONS` alone would move the
number substantially) was largely falsified by the 3-rep re-run. This one gets the same
treatment.

## Probe result (n=1, 2026-08-11, post-sweep engine @ fc795a38, fixed meter)

**Surfaced 1/7 (0.143) · defensible 5/7 · oracle 1.00 · $1.82 · 2.75M tokens.**

Against the pre-registered criteria:

- **secondary — FAIL**: 1/7 < 2.33/7. Surfacing went *down* vs the post-arm mean (n=1).
- **guard — PASS**: defensible 5/7, oracle 1.00, and the run was *cheaper* (2.75M tokens
  vs 3.9–4.9M — the sweep did not add ceremony cost).
- **primary — split, and the split is the finding.** Strictly by the scorer: no hit.
  But the bundle is qualitatively unlike all six predecessors: `gives:` enumerates the
  GET surfaces, and every one of the four never-questioned items now has a *recorded,
  priced decision* in ASSUMPTIONS, written pre-freeze — first time in six runs. The
  sweep changed **which questions get asked**. It did not change:

  1. **The register.** The decisions are stated declaratively ("GET /bookings lists
     every booking regardless of status", "DELETE is permitted for any caller — spec
     defines no ownership check") — the same authoritative voice as a stated
     requirement. The scorer's speech-act rule (a marker construction: *the spec does
     not say*) is exactly what distinguishes a flagged unknown from a confident
     assertion, and these are assertions. One phrase — "spec defines no ownership
     check" — is arguably a morphological variant of the `does not define` marker
     (`defines no`), but crediting it would not change the verdict: the line bundles
     three items' anchors, so attribution ties and credits nobody.
  2. **The bundling.** A6 packs cancelled-visibility, cancel-authority, and waitlist
     filtering into one line covering S2,S3,S4 — despite the one-line-one-silence rule
     shipping in the very build under test. The instruction exists; the agent did not
     follow it. A skill-prose rule with no engine checkpoint is an instruction that
     does not happen — the same lesson that produced the sweep itself.
  3. **The answers.** `A-list-scope` shipped `all` and `A-cancel-authority` shipped
     `anyone` — both still guessed_wrong. The sweep forced the question; the agent
     answered it wrong, confidently, both times in the direction of *no restriction*.

The honest summary: the sweep moved the failure from "never asked" to "asked, answered
wrong, and stated as fact". That is progress for auditability (the wrong decision is
now on the record with a cost-if-wrong attached) and no progress on surfacing or
correctness. n=1.

## Probe 2 (n=1, 2026-08-11, register-scaffold engine @ 83e222a1)

The register moved into the scaffold (each dimension line starts from "the request does
not say …; taking <reading> -> <cost if wrong>"; trailing hint says one line, one
silence). Result: **surfaced 2/7 · defensible 5/7 · oracle 1.00 · $2.56 · 4.74M tokens.**

**What the register fixed — completely.** All five authored lines hold the frame: each
is single-topic, not-said-first, reading-then-cost, unbundled. `A-priority-vs-fifo`
surfaced for the first time in a probe ("the request does not say what an omitted
`priority` means for ordering"). Probe 1's two failure modes — declarative register and
bundling — did not recur.

**What moved instead — the toll found the cheapest remaining slot.** The bundle lists
ONE surface:

    gives: [ S1 the booking HTTP surface — POST/GET /bookings, GET/DELETE
             /bookings/{id}, GET /rooms/{room_id}/waitlist ]

Five endpoints collapsed into a single S1, so the sweep demanded 5 pairs instead of ~25
and every line covers S1 alone. The who/which questions were then answered once, about
the loud endpoint (A1 [who] is about token semantics on POST, not row visibility on
GET), and `A-list-scope` / `A-cancel-authority` shipped `all` / `anyone` — still wrong,
still never named. The enumeration rule ("every route × verb is a surface") stood in
phases/direction.md throughout, unenforced — the third consecutive demonstration that a
prose rule with no engine checkpoint does not happen.

**The sequence, stated plainly:**

    v3 sweep:      surfaces enumerated ✓ → answers declarative + bundled
    v4 register:   register held ✓, unbundled ✓ → surfaces collapsed to one

Each intervention closed the hole it aimed at; the evasion moved to the cheapest slot
the engine does not check. The remaining candidates for that slot are known: surface
granularity (engine-checkable only heuristically — an S-line carrying several
methods/paths), and the answers themselves (not checkable at all — that is what the
gate's probes are for). Two probes, $4.38, two distinct failure modes characterized.

## Probe 3 (n=1, 2026-08-11, granularity-checkpoint engine @ dc7cb64c) — campaign close

freeze now refuses an S entry naming several HTTP methods ("one surface per S id").
Result: **surfaced 2/7 · defensible 5/7 · oracle 1.00 · $3.46 · 6.90M tokens.**

**Every engine checkpoint held, live.** The bundle lists five single-method surfaces
(S1–S5, including both GET reads). The sweep refused the freeze twice on `order:S5` —
a real gap, caught and forced during authoring, exactly the progressive shape intended.
Eight register-framed assumption lines, all per-surface `covers:`.

**The design intent is now achieved — by hand-audit, not by the scorer.** Read the
artifact: A1 flags cancel-authority AND list-scope in the not-said register, pre-freeze
("the request does not say whether Authorization … restricts who may read/cancel a
booking; taking … no ownership check gates GET/DELETE — any caller may read or cancel
any booking -> cost: no per-tenant isolation"). A3 flags cancelled-visibility the same
way ("does not say which statuses reads act on; taking … including cancelled"). Six
runs ago these were silent guesses; now they are declared, priced, challengeable
readings. A hand audit scores this artifact ~5/7 surfaced.

**Why the scorer credits 2/7 — three mechanical reasons, recorded and NOT tuned away:**

1. The scaffold's own `;` separators are meter sentence boundaries, so the marker
   clause ("the request does not say …") and the anchor-bearing "taking …" clause
   split into different sentences. Measured on A1: 3 meter-sentences; the marker lands
   in [0], the anchors (`ownership`, `cancel any booking`) in [2].
2. Anchor morphology: "who may read/cancel a booking" matches no anchor
   ("who can cancel", "cancels a booking").
3. The blanket `[who] covers: S1..S5` line carries cancel-authority and list-scope
   anchors TOGETHER, so attribution would tie and credit nobody even if paired — the
   exact honest limit pre-registered in FORMAT §5 when the sweep shipped.

ambiguity.py's own rule governs here: after seeing which way a change moves the number,
the honest outcome is to record the metric's limit, not to loosen the detector. Three
of three meter adjustments this campaign were made under that bar; these three stay
unfixed and documented.

**Cost of rigor, measured:** the full ~25-pair sweep run costs $3.46 / 6.9M tokens vs
$2.56 / 4.7M collapsed and $1.82 / 2.75M pre-register — full ceremony is ~35–90% more
expensive than its evasions. The answers on the two who-items are still wrong
(`all` / `anyone`) — but they are now *declared* wrong readings a reviewer can veto at
the freeze, which is precisely the auditability claim and nothing more.

**Campaign verdict (3 probes, $7.84):** the engine can force the QUESTIONS
(checkpoints hold; prose rules do not — demonstrated three times), the scaffold can
force the REGISTER, and nothing in an artifact check can force the right ANSWERS.
Surfacing meter: 0.143 baseline → 0.286 final probes, with the artifact's hand-audited
truth well above what the meter can attribute. The v3.0.0-beta.1 release claim stays
auditability-only.

## Method notes

- Surfacing must precede the first *code* edit (`is_implementation_write`); ADD's freeze
  gate guarantees exactly that ordering by construction, so the meter and the method
  agree on when recognition counts.
- rep1 is the run to read for what a bad ADD run looks like: worst surfacing of its
  cohort *and* the only oracle failure (0.75) — one run, an anecdote, not a correlation.
- ADD costs 3.5–4× vanilla in dollars and 5.6–7× in tokens per run on this workload.
